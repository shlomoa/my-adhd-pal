# Smartwatch Biometrics for ADHD and PTSD Managements

For existing diagnoses of ADHD and PTSD, smartwatch biometrics work best as real-time early-warning systems and symptom trackers to help you spot physiological shifts before they become overwhelming.
Here is how specific smartwatch metrics map to day-to-day symptom management for both conditions:

1. Real-Time Nervous System Alerts (PTSD & Emotional Dysregulation)

• High Heart Rate Alerts (at rest): Most smartwatches let you set a threshold for an elevated heart rate after 10 minutes of inactivity. A sudden spike while sitting still often flags an oncoming hyperarousal episode, trigger response, or panic attack before you consciously register the emotional shift.
• Stress / Body Battery Scores: Devices like Garmin, Fitbit, and Samsung calculate continuous stress scores using Heart Rate Variability (HRV) and heart rate. Watching this score climb rapidly during the day can signal sensory overload (ADHD) or hypervigilance fatigue (PTSD), giving you a cue to step away or regulate.
• Electrodermal Activity (EDA) / Skin Conductance: Available on select watches (such as certain Fitbit and Google Pixel models), micro-sweat sensors detect subtle sympathetic nervous system spikes, flagging acute stress or emotional overwhelm in real time.

2. Sleep & Nighttime Symptom Tracking

• Nighttime Heart Rate & Movement Spikes: Sudden heart rate surges paired with restless movement during sleep often correlate with trauma-related nightmares or nocturnal panic (PTSD), even if you do not fully remember waking up. Apps like NightWare (for Apple Watch) specifically use these biometric flags to vibrate and interrupt nightmares without fully waking you.
• Sleep Timing & REM/Deep Sleep Consistency: Tracking bedtime drift and fragmented sleep helps flag delayed sleep phase patterns common in ADHD, allowing you to adjust evening wind-down routines or time-sensitive habits before sleep debt worsens focus the next day.

3. Pacing, Medication, and Energy Management

• Medication Response & Wear-Off (ADHD): If you take stimulant medication, tracking your resting heart rate and HRV trends throughout the day can show when the medication is peaking and when a "rebound" or crash is occurring, which often mimics anxiety or irritability.
• Activity Slumps & Crash Detection: Prolonged drops in daily step counts or flattened movement curves can serve as an objective flag for PTSD avoidance/shutdown or ADHD burnout, helping you distinguish physical exhaustion from executive dysfunction.
